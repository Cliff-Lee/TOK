The distance formula is just Pythagoras extended into three perpendicular directions.

Worked example. Find the distance and midpoint between \(A(1,2,3)\) and \(B(5,8,6)\).

\[ AB=\sqrt{(5-1)^2+(8-2)^2+(6-3)^2} =\sqrt{4^2+6^2+3^2} =\sqrt{61} \approx 7.81. \] \[ M=\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\frac{2+8}{2},\frac{3+6}{2}\right)=(3,5,4.5). \]

Formula memory map

Right pyramid

\(V=\frac13Ah\), where \(A\) is base area. For square base \(s\), \(V=\frac13s^2h\).

Right cone

\(V=\frac13\pi r^2h\). Curved surface area \(=\pi r l\), where \(l\) is slant height.

Sphere

\(V=\frac43\pi r^3\). Surface area \(=4\pi r^2\).

Hemisphere

Volume \(=\frac23\pi r^3\). Curved area \(=2\pi r^2\). Total area \(=3\pi r^2\).

Surface area and nets

Surface area is about exposed faces. A cone and pyramid need slant height, not just vertical height.

Slant height matters

Cone. Radius \(r=5\), vertical height \(h=12\). Find slant height and total surface area.

First find the slant height: \[ l=\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13. \] Total surface area: \[ \pi r^2+\pi rl=25\pi+65\pi=90\pi. \]

Square pyramid. Base side \(s=10\), vertical height \(h=12\). Find the slant height of a triangular face.

Use the right triangle from the centre of the base to the midpoint of a side: \[ l=\sqrt{12^2+5^2}=13. \] Lateral area is \(4\left(\frac12 \cdot 10\cdot 13\right)=260\). Total surface area is \(260+10^2=360\).
In surface area questions, ask: which pieces of the net are included?

Right triangle decision guide

Need a length?

Use Pythagoras when two sides of a right triangle are known.

Need an angle?

Use \(\sin\), \(\cos\), or \(\tan\) based on opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse.

Line-plane angle?

Use the line and its projection on the plane.

Two-stage problem?

Often find a base diagonal first, then use a second triangle.

Angle calculation

Line-plane angle
\[ \tan\theta=\frac{\text{vertical height}}{\text{projection length}} \]

Example. A support cable runs from the ground to a point 12 m high. Its ground projection is 16 m. Find the angle between the cable and the ground.

The cable, vertical height and ground projection form a right triangle. \[ \tan\theta=\frac{12}{16}=0.75 \] \[ \theta=\tan^{-1}(0.75)\approx36.9^\circ. \]
Do not confuse the angle with the vertical with the angle with the plane. They are complementary in this right triangle.

TOK: axioms and 3D geometry

TOK prompt. What is an axiomatic system?

An axiomatic system begins with accepted starting statements, then builds theorems using logical deduction. In geometry, ideas such as points, lines, planes and distance rely on assumptions about space. The question is whether these starting assumptions are self-evident, chosen for usefulness, or justified by experience.

Discussion. Are axioms self-evident to everybody?

Euclidean geometry may feel self-evident in everyday architecture and design, but other geometries are useful in navigation, relativity and curved spaces. This suggests that axioms can be intuitive in one context but not universal.

Technology instructions

  1. For 3D distance, enter \(\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}\) carefully with brackets.
  2. For midpoint, calculate each coordinate separately by averaging: \((x_1+x_2)/2\), \((y_1+y_2)/2\), \((z_1+z_2)/2\).
  3. For angles, set the calculator to degree mode unless the question asks otherwise.
  4. Use inverse trig: \(\tan^{-1}\), \(\sin^{-1}\), or \(\cos^{-1}\) after identifying opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse.
  5. For volume and surface area, store repeated values such as \(r\), \(h\) and \(l\) if the calculator allows it.
  6. Check units: lengths in cm, areas in cm\(^2\), volumes in cm\(^3\).
Many errors in this topic are not calculator errors; they are diagram errors. Label the triangle first.

Worked examples

Example 1. A cuboid has dimensions \(6,8,10\). Find the space diagonal.

\[ d=\sqrt{6^2+8^2+10^2}=\sqrt{200}=10\sqrt2\approx14.1. \]

Example 2. Find the volume of a cone with \(r=4\), \(h=9\).

\[ V=\frac13\pi r^2h=\frac13\pi(4^2)(9)=48\pi\approx150.8. \]

Example 3. A line has vertical height \(5\) and projection length \(12\). Find its angle with the plane.

\[ \tan\theta=\frac{5}{12} \] \[ \theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{12}\right)\approx22.6^\circ. \]

Exam checklist

3D coordinates: subtract matching coordinates.
Midpoint: average each coordinate separately.
SA vs volume: square units vs cubic units.
Slant height: needed for cones and pyramid faces.
Angle: use the correct projection or triangle.
Composite: add volumes; count only exposed surfaces.

Suggested lesson flow

  1. Start with the cuboid diagonal explorer to show 3D problems as two right triangles.
  2. Move to 3D distance and midpoint using coordinates.
  3. Introduce solid formulae with dynamic diagrams and dimensions.
  4. Use nets and slant height examples to prevent surface-area errors.
  5. Train students to identify hidden right triangles in boxes, cones, pyramids and line-plane angle problems.
  6. Use composite solids to practise exposed vs hidden surfaces.
  7. Finish with context questions and the interactive question generator.

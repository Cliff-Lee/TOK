The big idea

A matrix usually turns and stretches a vector.

An eigenvector is a non-zero vector whose direction is not changed. It may be stretched, squashed, reversed, or left alone:

Av = λv

The number λ is the scale factor along that special direction.

2. Algebra beside the graph

The graph asks: Which directions keep their line? The algebra asks the same thing in symbols.

From geometry to algebra

If v is an eigenvector, the transformed vector Av is just a scalar multiple of v:

Av = λv

Move everything to one side:

Av − λIv = 0
(A − λI)v = 0

For a non-zero solution, the matrix must be singular:

det(A − λI) = 0

The compact 2×2 shortcut

For

A = [[a, b], [c, d]]

let

m = (a+d)/2    and    p = det(A) = ad − bc

Then

λ = m ± √(m² − p)

Same eigenvector, different scalings

Eigenvectors describe a direction, not one fixed arrow. The forms v, 2v, −v, v/||v||, or a version whose entries sum to 1 are all the same eigen-direction, as long as the vector is not zero.

5. Repeated transformations: why eigenvectors matter

Eigenvectors are especially useful when a matrix is applied again and again.

If Av = λv, then A²v = λ²v, and Aⁿv = λⁿv.

This explains why eigenvalues appear in Markov chains, population models, differential equations, computer graphics, vibration analysis and Google-style ranking algorithms.

Use the repeated Aⁿv checkbox in the first graph.
If one eigenvalue is much larger in size than the other, many starting vectors are eventually pulled toward the dominant eigen-direction.

Eigenbasis decomposition

7. Common misconceptions

“The eigenvector is unique.”

No. Any non-zero scalar multiple of an eigenvector is also an eigenvector.

“The zero vector counts.”

No. The zero vector is excluded because it is always mapped to zero and gives no direction.

“Complex eigenvalues are a mistake.”

No. They mean the real plane has no invariant line; the transformation includes rotation-like behaviour.

“Negative λ means impossible.”

No. A negative eigenvalue means the vector is reversed as well as scaled.

“Repeated eigenvalue means two eigenvectors.”

Not always. A repeated eigenvalue may have one eigenline, two independent eigenlines, or every direction.

“det(A − λI) = 0 is magic.”

It simply forces a non-zero solution to a homogeneous system.

What should students notice?

Discovery question:
When the square gets bigger or smaller, what happens to the two legs and the hypotenuse?
Key idea

The two legs are always equal. The hypotenuse is always the leg multiplied by √2.

Ratio to remember

leg : leg : hypotenuse = 1 : 1 : √2

The diagonal of a square is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. If each leg is ℓ, then by Pythagoras:
ℓ² + ℓ² = h², so 2ℓ² = h², therefore h = ℓ√2.

What should students notice?

Discovery question:
If the hypotenuse is the side of the equilateral triangle, why is the short leg half as long?
Key idea

The altitude splits the equilateral triangle exactly in half. So the short leg is half the hypotenuse.

Ratio to remember

short leg : long leg : hypotenuse = 1 : √3 : 2

If the hypotenuse is h, then the short leg is h ÷ 2. The long leg is found using Pythagoras:
long² + short² = h², so long = short × √3.

One-line plan

1. Name the triangle type.
2. Name the known side.
3. Use the correct ratio.
4. Check if your answer should be bigger or smaller.

Student Prompts

Mild: How can the special right triangle ratios help me find a missing side quickly?
Medium: When special right triangles are joined together, how can I pass a length from one triangle to the next?
Spicy: How can I prove my solution is correct and fix any mistake in the ratios I used?

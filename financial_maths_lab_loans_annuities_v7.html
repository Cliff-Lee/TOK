What each TI‑Nspire Finance Solver parameter means

TI‑Nspire essentials

Open a Calculator page, then use Menu → Finance → Finance Solver. Enter all known values, highlight the unknown value, and press Enter.

N — number of periods

Total number of payment or compounding periods.

25 years monthly → N = 25 × 12 = 300.

I% — annual interest rate

The quoted annual percentage rate. Do not divide by 100.

5% p.a. → enter I% = 5, not 0.05.

PV — present value

The starting value: amount borrowed, invested, or available now.

A $100 000 loan usually uses PV = 100000.

PMT — regular payment

The equal payment made each period.

Monthly repayment, monthly deposit, pension withdrawal.

FV — future value

The value at the end of the model.

For a fully repaid loan, FV = 0.

P/Y — payments per year

How many regular payments are made each year.

Monthly payments → P/Y = 12.

C/Y — compounds per year

How many times interest is added each year.

Usually set C/Y equal to P/Y in this course.

PMT: END — payment timing

Payments occur at the end of each period.

This is the normal exam assumption for annuities.

Solve — unknown value

Leave the unknown field blank or move the cursor to it, then press Enter.

Find FV, PV, PMT, N, or I% depending on the question.

Cash-flow sign convention: money going out and money coming in should have opposite signs. For example, an investment might use PV = −5000 and produce FV = 5229.85. A loan might use PV = 100000 and produce PMT = −584.59. In written answers, usually report the positive amount: “$584.59 per month.”

1. Discover compound growth

Within syllabus

Students first fill a table and see why compound interest becomes a geometric sequence.

Reveal key idea

Simple interest adds the same amount each year. Compound interest multiplies by the same factor each year. That makes the compound balances a geometric sequence.

Simple: PV + PVr100t
Compound: PV(1 + r100)t

TI‑Nspire preview

Open a Calculator page, then use:

  1. Menu → Finance → Finance Solver
  2. For compound interest with no regular payments, enter PMT = 0.
  3. For annual compounding, set P/Y = 1 and C/Y = 1.
  4. Highlight the unknown variable, then press Enter to solve.

2. Compound interest and depreciation calculator

Within syllabus
TI‑Nspire setup: N = compounding periods × years, I% = annual rate, PMT = 0, P/Y = C/Y = compounding periods per year. Use a negative I% for depreciation if using the Finance Solver.

Worked example: investment

Question: $5000 is invested at 3% p.a. compounded monthly for 18 months.

N = 18,  I% = 3,  PV = −5000,  PMT = 0,  P/Y = C/Y = 12
FV ≈ 5229.85

Answer: $5229.85.

Worked example: depreciation

Question: A car worth £20 000 depreciates by 15% each year for 5 years.

FV = 20000(1 − 0.15)5 = 20000(0.85)5

Answer: £8874.11, so about £8870 to the nearest £10.

3. Inflation and real value

Within syllabus

Nominal value is the amount of money shown. Real value adjusts for inflation, so it estimates purchasing power.

Exam-friendly approximation

For small percentages, use the approximate real annual percentage change:

real rate ≈ nominal rate − inflation rate

This is quick, intuitive, and useful for interpreting exam contexts.

Extension: exact adjustment

The exact real growth factor divides the nominal growth factor by the inflation growth factor.

real factor = 1 + c/1001 + i/100

Useful for accuracy discussions, modelling, hyperinflation, and TOK-style assumptions.

6. Examination practice

Revealable worked solutions

Questions deliberately mix contexts so students practise choosing the financial variable, not just repeating a procedure.

Syllabus

1. Future value

$7500 is invested at 4.8% p.a. compounded quarterly for 6 years. Find the balance.

Reveal solution

N = 24, quarterly rate = 4.8% ÷ 4 = 1.2%.

FV = 7500(1.012)24 = 9986.05

Answer: $9986.05.

Syllabus

2. Depreciation

Equipment worth $22 000 depreciates by 18% per year for 7 years. Estimate its value.

Reveal solution
FV = 22000(0.82)7 = 5484.28

Answer: about $5484.

Syllabus

3. Time required

$3000 is invested at 3.2% p.a. compounded monthly. How long until it reaches $5000?

Reveal solution

Use TVM: PV = −3000, FV = 5000, I% = 3.2, PMT = 0, P/Y = C/Y = 12.

N ≈ 191.8 months ≈ 16.0 years

Answer: about 16 years.

Syllabus

4. Required rate

$4000 must double in 12 years, compounded monthly. Find the annual nominal interest rate.

Reveal solution

Use TVM: N = 144, PV = −4000, FV = 8000, PMT = 0, P/Y = C/Y = 12.

I% ≈ 5.79

Answer: 5.79% p.a. compounded monthly.

Syllabus

5. Real value

$12 000 is invested for 8 years at 5.5% p.a. Inflation is 2.8% p.a. Estimate the real value.

Reveal solution

Approximate real rate = 5.5 − 2.8 = 2.7%.

Real value ≈ 12000(1.027)8 = 14850.63

Answer: about $14 851. Exact model gives $14 765.77.

PMT ≈ −1079.00

Exam modelling

9. Compare two mortgages

A $250 000 mortgage has two options. A: 4.0% p.a., monthly repayments, 25 years. B: 4.6% p.a., monthly repayments, 30 years. Both compound monthly. Which has lower monthly payments? Which has lower total repayment?

Reveal solution

A: N = 300, I% = 4.0, PV = 250000, P/Y = C/Y = 12.

PMTA ≈ 1319.59,  totalA ≈ 395877.63

B: N = 360, I% = 4.6, PV = 250000, P/Y = C/Y = 12.

PMTB ≈ 1281.61,  totalB ≈ 461379.93

Answer: B has lower monthly payments, but A has lower total repayment.

10. Savings annuity

$250 is deposited at the end of each month into an account paying 6% p.a. compounded monthly for 5 years. Find the future value.

Reveal solution

Monthly rate = 0.5%, N = 60.

FV = 250(1.005)60 − 10.005 = 17442.51

Answer: $17 442.51.

11. Changing interest rate

A $18 000 car loan is repaid at $350 per month. The first 2 years are at 5.2% p.a.; the remaining 3 years are at 6.4% p.a. Both compound monthly. Can the borrower finish in 5 years?

Reveal solution

After 24 payments, balance is about $11 136.11. Required payment for the next 36 months at 6.4% is:

PMT ≈ 340.80

Answer: Yes. $350 per month is enough, with a slightly smaller final adjustment.

12. Approximate vs exact real growth

Government spending rises by 5% per year while inflation is 2.5% per year. Find the real percentage increase over 5 years using the approximate and exact models.

Reveal solution

Approximate annual real rate = 2.5%.

Approx factor = 1.0255 = 1.1314 → 13.14% increase
Exact factor = 1.051.0255 = 1.1280 → 12.80% increase

7. Extension: step-by-step derivations

Beyond routine syllabus

These derivations all go back to one central idea: compound interest creates a geometric sequence. Students can reveal one stage at a time, using each hint before seeing the next line.

Teacher use: keep this as an enrichment or “why the calculator works” section. The syllabus emphasises technology for annuities and amortization, but these derivations help students understand what the Finance Solver is doing.

Annuity future value from a geometric sequence

Question behind the formula: if the same amount is paid at the end of each period, how much is the account worth after the final payment?

Reveal stages Goal: derive the future value of regular deposits.
Hint 1

Think about each payment separately. The final payment earns no interest. The payment before it earns interest for one period. The first payment earns interest for the most periods.

Stage 2: list the payments

Let each payment be R, the interest rate per period be i, and the number of payments be n.

R(1+i)n−1 R(1+i)n−2 ... R(1+i) R
Stage 3: write the sum
FV = R(1+i)n−1 + R(1+i)n−2 + ... + R(1+i) + R

This is a geometric series because each term is found by multiplying or dividing by the same factor.

Hint 4

The common ratio is 1+i. It is easier to write the same sum in ascending powers:

FV = R[1 + (1+i) + (1+i)2 + ... + (1+i)n−1]
Stage 5: use the geometric-series formula

For a finite geometric series:

1 + q + q2 + ... + qn−1 = qn − 1q − 1

Here, q = 1+i.

Final result
FV = R(1+i)n − 1(1+i) − 1 = R(1+i)n − 1i
Meaning: this gives the value of equal payments made at the end of each period, once all the payments and interest have accumulated.

Loan payment formula from a geometric sequence

Question behind the formula: what equal payment A will exactly pay off a loan of P after n periods?

Reveal stages Goal: derive the repayment formula.
Hint 1

A loan repayment formula works by asking: what is the present value of all future payments?

If the loan is exactly paid off, then:

present value of all repayments = principal borrowed
Stage 2: discount each payment

A payment of A one period from now is worth less today. Its present value is:

A1+i

A payment two periods from now has present value:

A(1+i)2
Stage 3: write the present-value sum
P = A1+i + A(1+i)2 + ... + A(1+i)n
Hint 4

This is also a geometric series, but this time the common ratio is:

q = 11+i

So the powers are q, q2, ..., qn.

Stage 5: factor out A
P = A[q + q2 + ... + qn]

The finite sum is:

q + q2 + ... + qn = q(1 − qn)1 − q
Stage 6: substitute q
P = A1 − (1+i)−ni

Now solve for A.

Final result
A = Pi1 − (1+i)−n
Meaning: this is the regular payment needed to reduce the loan balance to zero after n payments.

Equivalent annual rate from repeated multiplication

A quoted annual rate compounded monthly is not exactly the same as the actual one-year growth.

Reveal stages Goal: convert a nominal rate into the actual annual growth.
Hint 1

Start with the normal compound interest multiplier. If the annual nominal rate is r% and there are m compounding periods per year, then the rate per period is:

r100m
Stage 2: one-period multiplier
one-period multiplier = 1 + r100m
Stage 3: multiply for one full year

There are m periods in one year, so the one-year multiplier is:

annual multiplier = (1 + r100m)m
Stage 4: convert multiplier to percentage rate

A multiplier of 1.1268 means a 12.68% increase. So subtract 1 to get the effective annual rate as a decimal.

effective annual rate = (1 + r100m)m − 1
Example

For 12% p.a. compounded monthly, r = 12 and m = 12.

(1 + 12100 × 12)12 − 1 = 0.1268...
Answer: the effective annual rate is about 12.68%.

Continuous compounding as a limiting geometric process

This is a conceptual extension. It shows how the compound-interest formula leads naturally to the exponential constant e.

Reveal stages Goal: connect increasingly frequent compounding to e.
Hint 1

For compound interest with m compounding periods per year:

FV = PV(1 + rm)mt

Here r is written as a decimal, for example 5% becomes 0.05.

Stage 2: ask what happens as compounding gets more frequent

Yearly, monthly, daily, hourly, every second... the value approaches a limiting multiplier.

m = 1 m = 12 m = 365 m → ∞
Stage 3: use the definition of e

The key limit is:

(1 + xn)n → ex as n → ∞
Stage 4: apply it to compound interest

As m becomes infinitely large:

(1 + rm)mt → ert
Final result
FV = PV ert
Meaning: continuous compounding is the limiting case of a geometric growth process where the compounding period becomes infinitely small.

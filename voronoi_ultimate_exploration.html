Why perpendicular bisectors?

For two sites A and B, the boundary is the line containing every point equally far from A and B.

Distance to A = Distance to B

This line passes through the midpoint of AB and is perpendicular to AB.

Construction steps

  1. Join a pair of sites with a line segment.
  2. Find the midpoint of that segment.
  3. Draw the perpendicular bisector.
  4. Keep only the parts of boundaries that actually separate nearest regions.

Algebra of one boundary

Exam warning: drawing every perpendicular bisector gives too many lines. A final Voronoi diagram keeps only the edges of the cells.
The new site is added after the first four sites. The algorithm traces only the cell of the new site, then updates the diagram.

Incremental algorithm

The incremental algorithm adds one site at a time. Instead of redrawing everything, it finds the new cell and cuts away the old cells around it.

Core version

  1. Start with a completed diagram for the old sites.
  2. Add the new site N.
  3. For each old site, draw the perpendicular bisector between N and that old site.
  4. The side containing N is kept. Intersect all these half-planes.
  5. The remaining polygon is the new cell for N.
  6. Update neighbouring cells by trimming them along the new edges.

Useful exam language

Boundary between A and B: points equidistant from A and B.
Cell of A: points closer to A than all other sites.
Voronoi vertex: point equidistant from three or more sites.
Largest empty circle: centre is often a Voronoi vertex or boundary/corner, then choose the greatest minimum distance.

Teacher notes: suggested sequence

Lesson 1: intuition
Use the first tab. Ask students to drag P and explain why boundaries are “decision lines”.
Lesson 2: construction
Use perpendicular bisectors. Move from visual equality of distance to midpoint + gradient algebra.
Lesson 3: algorithm
Use the incremental tab. Emphasise “keep the side containing the new site”.

Key vocabulary

WordStudent-friendly meaning
SiteThe fixed point, such as a fire station, airport, school, tower, or data station.
Cell / regionThe area closest to one site.
EdgeA boundary line segment between two cells. Points on it are equally close to two sites.
VertexA corner where edges meet. Often equally close to three or more sites.
Nearest-neighbour interpolationAssign a value to a location by using the value at the nearest site.
Largest empty circleThe largest circle that can fit without containing any sites inside it.

Assessment progression

  1. Identify the nearest site by inspection and distance.
  2. Explain what a cell, edge and vertex mean.
  3. Construct boundaries using perpendicular bisectors.
  4. Find an edge equation using midpoint and perpendicular gradient.
  5. Use nearest-neighbour interpolation in context.
  6. Reason about the largest empty circle / toxic waste style problem.

Sperner's Lemma Investigation

A large triangle is subdivided into many small triangles. Vertices are coloured using three colours, but the boundary has special rules.

Prompting Question

When the boundary colouring rules are followed, is it possible to avoid every small triangle having all three colours?

No solution shown
1. Understand the problem What are the rules?
  • What are the three corner colours?
  • What colours are allowed on each side of the large triangle?
  • What colours are allowed inside the triangle?
  • What would make a small triangle a three-colour triangle?
2. Make a plan How will you test the pattern?
  • Start with a small subdivision size.
  • Try different valid colourings.
  • Record the number of three-colour triangles.
  • Look for something that seems impossible to avoid.

4. Look back and justify Why might it always work?
  • Can you follow a path through the small triangles using two of the colours?
  • Where can such a path enter the large triangle?
  • Where could the path stop?
  • How could you convince someone without checking every possible colouring?
Extension question: What happens if one boundary rule is broken? Change the colouring mentally or on paper and predict whether the pattern must still happen.

1. Understand the problem What is being asked?
  • What shape are you investigating?
  • What can move?
  • What quantities can you measure?
  • What does it mean for something to be constant?
2. Make a plan How will you collect evidence?
  • Choose several different positions for point P.
  • Record the three perpendicular distances each time.
  • Look for a relationship between the measurements.
  • Decide how many examples are enough to make a conjecture.

4. Look back and justify Why might it always work?
  • Can you connect the three measured distances to areas?
  • Can the large triangle be split into smaller triangles?
  • Which lengths stay the same when P moves?
  • How could you convince someone without using more measurements?

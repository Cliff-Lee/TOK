Vector and powers

A matrix moves almost every vector by turning and stretching it. Eigenvectors are the rare directions that do not turn.

Diagonalisation is the idea of changing to the eigenvector viewpoint. In that viewpoint, the matrix action becomes simple: one eigen-direction is multiplied by \(\lambda_1\), and the other by \(\lambda_2\).

What to notice

  • Most arrows change direction.
  • An eigenvector stays on the same line.
  • The eigenvalue tells the stretch factor on that line.
  • If the eigenvalue is negative, the vector flips through the origin.

Student explanation

Instead of thinking in ordinary \(x\)- and \(y\)-directions, diagonalisation says: use the two eigenvectors as your new axes. Then the matrix is just two independent stretches.

The whole method comes from one idea: \(A\mathbf{x}=\lambda\mathbf{x}\).

If the columns of \(P\) are eigenvectors, multiplying by \(A\) has the same effect as multiplying each column by its eigenvalue.

Build \(P\) and \(D\)

The key chain

  1. Start with eigenvectors.
    \(A\mathbf{x}_1=\lambda_1\mathbf{x}_1\) and \(A\mathbf{x}_2=\lambda_2\mathbf{x}_2\).
  2. Put them into a matrix.
    \(P=(\mathbf{x}_1\ \mathbf{x}_2)\).
  3. Write both equations at once.
    \(AP=PD\).
  4. Rearrange.
    \(A=PDP^{-1}\).

The payoff: powers become easy.

Directly calculating \(A^n\) can be slow. But if \(A=PDP^{-1}\), then the middle terms cancel when we multiply repeatedly.

Power formula

Show why the cancellation works
\[ A^n=(PDP^{-1})(PDP^{-1})\cdots(PDP^{-1}) \] The pairs \(P^{-1}P\) become \(I\), so only one \(P\), many \(D\)'s, and one \(P^{-1}\) remain: \[ A^n=PD^nP^{-1}. \]

Diagonalisation explains long-term behaviour.

Matrix powers appear whenever the same linear rule is repeated: migration, population movement, Markov-style transitions, economic exchange, and some discrete models of interacting quantities.

Discrete model: repeated movement

Suppose \(u_k\) stores two quantities at step \(k\), such as people in two towns, two account balances, or two interacting populations.

\[ u_{k+1}=Au_k,\qquad u_k=A^k u_0=PD^kP^{-1}u_0 \]

Use the vector controls on the left as \(u_0\). The table in the Powers section shows the evolution.

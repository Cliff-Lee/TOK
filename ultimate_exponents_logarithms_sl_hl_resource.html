SL 1.5

Laws of exponents with integer exponents

Use, explain, and simplify expressions involving positive, zero, and negative integer exponents.

\[a^m a^n=a^{m+n},\quad \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\quad (a^m)^n=a^{mn}\]
SL 1.5

Introduction to logarithms

Understand logs as the inverse question for exponentials, especially base 10 and base \(e\).

\[a^x=b \iff \log_a(b)=x,\qquad \log_e x=\ln x\]
AHL 1.10

Rational exponents

Simplify numerical and algebraic expressions involving fractional and negative fractional exponents.

\[a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a},\quad a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}=(\sqrt[n]{a})^m\]

1. Explore first: where powers appear

Students begin with situations, not rules. The aim is to make powers feel like a compact language for repeated multiplication, scaling, and repeated percentage change.

Both

Sound level

“Why can a sound-scale jump feel huge even when the number only rises by 10?”

Scientific notation

“How can we compare atoms, oceans, planets, and viruses without writing endless zeros?”

Roots as powers

“If volume is \(s^3\), why is side length \(V^{1/3}\)?”

2. Discover and formalise the exponent laws

Move from expanded multiplication to general rules. The examples use letters only after the pattern is clear with numbers.

SL

Interactive law builder

Choose a base and two exponents. The expansion shows why the rule works.

Key rules students need

\[a^m a^n=a^{m+n}\]
\[\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}\quad (a\ne0)\]
\[(a^m)^n=a^{mn}\]
\[(ab)^n=a^n b^n,\qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}\]
Important: These are not “magic laws”. They are shortcuts for repeated multiplication. When stuck, expand a small example.

Zero and negative exponents as a ladder

Each step down divides by the base. That is why negative powers become fractions.

Algebra example: guide students, do not just tell them

1

Separate coefficients and variables

\(2x^3\cdot 7x^4=(2\cdot7)(x^3\cdot x^4)\)

2

Use the product law

\(x^3x^4=x^{3+4}=x^7\)

3

Write the simplified expression

\(2x^3\cdot 7x^4=14x^7\)

3. Standard form: powers of 10 as a language for scale

This section links exponent laws to very large and very small quantities. It works well before logarithms because students see why base 10 is special.

SL

Convert a number into standard form

Why it matters

  • Comparison: compare exponents first, then coefficients.
  • Calculation: multiply coefficients and add powers of 10.
  • Modelling: represent atoms, planets, money, bacteria, distances, and tiny probabilities.
\[(3.2\times10^5)(4\times10^3)=12.8\times10^8=1.28\times10^9\]

4. Logarithms: the inverse question

A logarithm asks: “What exponent do I need?” This is the conceptual bridge from exponent laws to solving exponential equations.

SL

The exponential/log machine

Three meanings students should connect

1

Exponential form

\(10^3=1000\)

2

Logarithmic form

\(\log_{10}(1000)=3\)

3

Sentence form

“The power of 10 needed to make 1000 is 3.”

Base 10: common logs

Useful when quantities are measured in powers of 10.

\[\log(1000)=3\]

Base \(e\): natural logs

Useful for continuous growth and decay.

\[\log_e(x)=\ln(x)\]

Contexts

Decibels, Richter scale, pH, compound growth, cooling, and half-life all make logarithms feel necessary rather than arbitrary.

Mini context: pH, sound, and earthquakes

pH uses \(-\log_{10}\) of hydrogen ion concentration. A pH decrease of 1 means the concentration is multiplied by 10.

\[\text{pH}=-\log_{10}[H^+]\]

Sound level compares intensity to a reference intensity using a logarithm. A ratio becomes an additive scale.

\[\beta=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)\]

Earthquake magnitude is logarithmic. A difference of 1 in magnitude represents a much larger physical intensity.

\[\text{larger log value} \Rightarrow \text{many times larger shaking}\]

6. HL extension: rational exponents

HL students extend the exponent laws from integers to fractions. The important idea is that roots can be written as powers, so exponent laws still work.

Guided proof: why \(a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}\)?

Reveal each clue and ask students to finish the next sentence.

?

Start

What does \(\sqrt[n]{a}\) mean?

Positive fractional exponents

\[32^{3/5}=(\sqrt[5]{32})^3=2^3=8\]

Negative fractional exponents

\[x^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{x^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\]

\[5^{1/2}\cdot5^{1/3}=5^{1/2+1/3}=5^{5/6}\]

8. Misconception checker

Students often know the rules but apply them in the wrong situations. This section deliberately targets common errors.

Both

Which statements are always true?

Error diagnosis routine

  1. Ask: Are we multiplying powers, dividing powers, raising a power, or adding terms?
  2. Check: Are the bases the same?
  3. Expand: Try a small example such as \(2^3\cdot2^2\).
  4. State: Which law was used?
  5. Verify: Calculator check only after the algebraic structure is clear.
Powerful teacher move: Ask students to create a wrong solution and then explain why it is wrong.

9. One-page summary

A compact reference for revision. Use the view-mode buttons to print SL only, HL only, or the full version.

Revision

SL must-know ideas

  • Exponent laws are shortcuts for repeated multiplication.
  • \(a^0=1\) for \(a\ne0\).
  • \(a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}\).
  • Standard form writes numbers as \(a\times10^k\), where \(1\le a<10\).
  • \(a^x=b\) is equivalent to \(\log_a b=x\).
  • \(\log_e x=\ln x\).

HL must-know ideas

  • \(a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}\).
  • \(a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}=(\sqrt[n]{a})^m\).
  • Negative fractional exponents combine roots and reciprocals.
  • Same-base laws still work with rational exponents.
  • Check domain restrictions when even roots are involved.
Best learning sequence: Context → pattern → expanded multiplication → rule → algebraic fluency → calculator check → exam-style communication.

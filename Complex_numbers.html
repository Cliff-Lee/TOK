Concise consolidation notes

\(z=a+bi\), where \(a=\operatorname{Re}(z)\), \(b=\operatorname{Im}(z)\), and \(i^2=-1\). The conjugate is \(\bar z=a-bi\).

\((a+bi)+(c+di)=(a+c)+(b+d)i\)

\((a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i\)

\(\dfrac{a+bi}{c+di}=\dfrac{(a+bi)(c-di)}{c^2+d^2}\), provided \(c+di\ne0\).

\(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). The argument \(\arg z=\theta\) is the angle from the positive real axis to the vector \((a,b)\). Use the quadrant, not just \(\tan^{-1}(b/a)\).

\(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=re^{i\theta}\), where \(r=|z|\). In Cartesian form, \(a=r\cos\theta\), \(b=r\sin\theta\).

If \(z=r e^{i\theta}\) and \(w=s e^{i\phi}\), then \(zw=rs e^{i(\theta+\phi)}\), \(\dfrac zw=\dfrac rs e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\), and \(z^n=r^n e^{in\theta}\).

Addition/subtraction are vector addition/subtraction. Multiplication by \(r e^{i\theta}\) is a stretch by scale factor \(r\) and a rotation by \(\theta\) about the origin.

For \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), \(x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\). If \(b^2-4ac<0\), the two roots are a conjugate pair.

Represent \(A\cos(t+\alpha)\) by the phasor \(A e^{i\alpha}\). Add phasors, then convert the result \(R e^{i\phi}\) back to \(R\cos(t+\phi)\).

Exam traps and calculator reminders

Common traps

Forgetting the quadrant for \(\arg z\); leaving answers in degrees when radians are requested; using Cartesian form for a high power; forgetting to multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate; treating \(\sqrt{-a}\sqrt{-b}=\sqrt{ab}\) as a general rule.

TI-Nspire style workflow

Set complex format to rectangular or polar as needed. Use cSolve or complex mode for quadratics with no real roots. Use functions such as abs(z), angle(z), conj(z), real(z), and imag(z). For high powers, convert to polar/Euler form first when exact reasoning is needed.

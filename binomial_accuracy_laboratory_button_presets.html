Stage 1: patterns before formula

Build the idea from a geometric series

Start with a familiar infinite series. When |x| < 1, 1 + x + x² + x³ + ... = 1 / (1 - x). That means (1 - x)-1 has an infinite expansion.

(1−x) −1 = 1 +x +x2 +x3 +x4 +⋯

Now multiply two copies of this series. The coefficient of each power counts the number of ways to make that power.

From pattern to generalization

For any rational exponent n, the generalized binomial expansion is:

(1+u) n = 1 +nu + n(n−1)u2 2! + n(n−1)(n−2)u3 3! +⋯

The key condition is usually |u| < 1. If the expression is not already in the form (1 + u)^n, factor first.

What to notice

Teacher reveal: why accuracy changes

The size of the inner value u = kx is crucial. When |u| is small, later terms become tiny quickly. When |u| is close to 1, convergence is slow. When |u| ≥ 1, the generalized binomial series is not valid in the usual interval, so adding more terms may not improve the approximation.

Teacher Notes: learning progression

This laboratory follows the sequence: discovery through patterns → reliable procedure → approximation and convergence.

Suggested lesson flow

  1. Discover: Students build negative-power expansions from geometric series and coefficient patterns.
  2. Generalize: Introduce (1+u)^n and the condition |u|<1.
  3. Method: Practise factorising into A(1+kx)^n, expanding, and stating validity.
  4. Accuracy: Investigate how the number of terms affects absolute error.
  5. Reflect: Students explain why smaller |u| gives faster convergence.

Exit ticket questions

  1. Why is |u|<1 more important than just |x|<1?
  2. Why does (1+x)^5 stop but (1+x)^{-1} continues forever?
  3. For a fixed number of terms, why is x=0.1 usually more accurate than x=0.8?
  4. How could you use the binomial expansion to approximate √1.04?
